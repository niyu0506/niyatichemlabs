## About this formulation

In ayurveda, diabetes is understood as a *kapha*-type metabolic disorder in which diminished
*agni* (digestive fire) leads to a tendency toward high blood sugar. Classical texts classify
twenty-four forms of the condition under *Prameha*. Diabopan Tablet brings together sixteen
herbs traditionally used in that context, standardised and combined in a single coated tablet.

## The herbs, and why they are here

**Gudmar (Gymnema sylvestre)** — one of the most widely used herbs in this category. It acts
indirectly, through stimulation of insulin secretion, rather than on carbohydrate metabolism
directly.

**Vijaysar (Pterocarpus marsupium)** — the bark contains pterostilbene, and the flavonoid
epicatechin extracted from it is traditionally associated with supporting healthy sugar levels.

**Karela (Momordica charantia)** — bitter gourd is rich in chromium and magnesium, both
micronutrients involved in normal sugar metabolism.

**Jambubeej (Syzygium cumini)** — jamun seed, long used in Indian households for supporting
fasting blood sugar levels.

**Mamejva (Enicostemma littorale)** — traditionally associated with insulin sensitivity and
with supporting normal lipid levels.

**Methi (Trigonella foenum-graecum)** — rich in dietary fibre, and a well-established
anti-inflammatory herb in ayurvedic practice.

**Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia)** — a classical *rasayana*, associated with antioxidant
activity and with supporting the body's own regulation of blood sugar.

**Neem (Azadirachta indica), Kadu (Picrorhiza kurroa), Kachaka (Caesalpinia crista)** — a
supporting group of bitter herbs, traditionally used to support liver function and healthy
metabolism.

**Shuddha Shilajit (Asphaltum)** — purified mineral pitch, used in ayurveda across
genito-urinary, digestive and metabolic indications.

**Belpatra (Aegle marmelos), Indrajav (Holarrhena antidysenterica), Gorakhmundi
(Sphaeranthus indicus)** — completing the formulation with herbs used across the Ayurvedic,
Unani and Siddha systems in India, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka.
