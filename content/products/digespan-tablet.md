## About this formulation

Ayurveda explains most digestive complaints through two ideas. The first is aggravation of the
*doshas* — *vata*, *pitta* and *kapha*. The second is *ama*, the residue left behind when food is
not fully digested. Seasonal change, repetitive diet, dust and pollution all encourage *ama* to
build up and adhere to the tissues, where it is understood to clog the *shrotas*, the channels
through which nutrition and oxygen reach the tissues and waste leaves them. Digespan Tablet
combines twelve classical digestives and mild eliminatives in a 600 mg tablet intended to keep
that residue moving rather than settling.

## The herbs, and why they are here

**Haritaki (Terminalia chebula)** — the largest ingredient at 125 mg. Its tannins and chebulic
acid make it a gentle, non-griping laxative, traditionally used for constipation and for toning
the colon.

**Triphala** — the classical three-fruit combination, among the most widely documented ayurvedic
preparations for colon cleansing and for digestion generally.

**Trikatu** — ginger, long pepper and black pepper together. Trikatu is warming and carminative,
used where there is loss of appetite, belching, nausea, flatulence or a sense of gastric
distension, and it improves the penetration of the other herbs.

**Ajwain (Trachyspermum ammi)** — carries thymol-rich essential oil, which gives the seed its
aroma and is associated with increased activity of the digestive enzymes of the intestinal
tract.

**Sunthee (Zingiber officinale)** — dry ginger, rich in proteolytic enzyme activity and
associated with increased bile production. Its shogaol and gingerol constituents account for its
traditional antiemetic use.

**Baelgarbha (Aegle marmelos)** — bael fruit pulp, containing alkaloids traditionally used to
protect the intestinal flora and support properly formed stools.

**Indrajav and Kutaj (Holarrhena antidysenterica)** — seed and bark of the same plant, with
antidysenteric, antidiarrhoeal and anti-amoebic properties recorded in classical use, including
for irritable bowel and liver complaints.

**Chitrak (Plumbago zeylanica)** — a strong digestive stimulant, used traditionally in dyspepsia,
colic and vitiated conditions of *vata* and *kapha*.

**Methi (Trigonella foenum-graecum)** — rich in dietary fibre and a well-established
anti-inflammatory herb in ayurvedic practice.

**Amlavetas (Garcinia pedunculata) and Hing (Ferula narthex)** — an astringent laxative and a
classical antispasmodic resin, completing the formulation.
