## About this formulation

Green tea is made from the leaf of *Camellia sinensis*, the same plant that yields black tea.
The difference lies entirely in processing: green tea leaf is steamed or pan-fired soon after
picking rather than allowed to oxidise, which preserves the polyphenol fraction that would
otherwise be converted during fermentation. The leaf can be taken as a beverage, or an extract
can be prepared from it for use in a measured dose.

Green Tea Capsule takes the second route. Each capsule contains 400 mg of dry leaf extract,
assayed to not less than 50% polyphenols, which gives a consistent intake that a cup of brewed
tea cannot reliably provide.

## The extract, and what it carries

**Polyphenols and catechins** — the constituent group by which the extract is standardised,
and the basis of green tea's long-standing use as an antioxidant. They are the reason the leaf
is processed unoxidised.

**Naturally occurring caffeine** — present in the leaf and carried into the extract. It
underlies the traditional use of green tea for mental alertness and for supporting physical
performance and fat metabolism during exercise.

**L-theanine** — an amino acid characteristic of tea leaf, associated with the calm quality of
alertness that distinguishes tea from other caffeinated drinks.

Green tea appears in a wide range of formulations for this reason: it sits equally well in a
slimming range, a general antioxidant range and a cognitive support range, and it is one of the
few botanicals a customer can put on a label with no explanation needed.
