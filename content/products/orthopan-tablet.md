## About this formulation

Orthopan Tablet brings together nine herbs that ayurveda has traditionally used where joints
and muscles are concerned. The three lead ingredients — Rasna, Shallaki and Guggul-family
resins — are the herbs most often named in classical texts for *sandhivata*, the vata-related
condition of the joints. Ashwagandha and Bala are included for musculoskeletal strength, and
Punarnava and Gokharu for their traditional diuretic role in clearing the tissues.

## The herbs, and why they are here

**Rasna (Vanda roxburghii)** — a long-standing herb of the indigenous system for rheumatic and
allied complaints. A decoction of the plant has traditionally been used where joints are stiff
or painful.

**Shallaki (Boswellia serrata)** — salai guggul. The oleo-gum resin is the most widely used
ayurvedic material for chronic joint discomfort, traditionally associated with easier movement
and reduced swelling, and suitable for extended use.

**Nirgundi (Vitex negundo)** — the leaf, used both internally and as an oil, is regarded in
ayurveda as one of the most dependable herbs for joint and muscular pain.

**Shuddha Guggul (Commiphora mukul)** — purified gum resin, traditionally used in rheumatic
conditions and in disorders of metabolism.

**Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera)** — an adaptogen with anti-inflammatory standing in
ayurveda, included here to support endurance and the body's response to physical stress.

**Bala (Sida cordifolia)** — the name means strength. Bala appears widely in ayurvedic
medicines and oils intended to support bones, muscles and joints.

**Gokharu (Pedalium murex)** — traditionally used for its diuretic action and in inflammatory
and genito-urinary conditions.

**Erandbeej (Ricinus communis)** — castor seed, classically associated with vata disorders and
with relief of muscular aches and back discomfort.

**Punarnava (Boerhaavia diffusa)** — used in ayurveda to support the clearing of fluid from the
tissues, and traditionally applied where joints are swollen or painful.
